0. What this report shows¶

A hate-speech detector that reads a tweet and an image is usually assumed to use both. This project audits one checkpoint, Amirhossein75/clip-vit-base-mmhs150k-mtl, and asks a narrower question than whether it is accurate: which modality does it actually read, and can the explanation methods that claim to show this be trusted?

The checkpoint reproduces its published metrics, so the audit concerns the model as released. Of seven candidate attribution methods, two clear a pre-registered faithfulness floor on text (LIME and KernelSHAP, both near six times the floor), and on the image side one clears it and barely (occlusion). Those three then play a coalition game over the two modalities on 3,000 samples. Text carries almost all of the decision: its Shapley value averages +0.047 against +0.0014 for the image, and it dominates on 99.4% of samples. The image is not inert: on 56.5% of samples its contribution is negative, so the picture often works against the label the text already implies. The fusion gate does not predict this split (§5), which rules the gate out as a shortcut explanation. Ablating the units an explanation blames flips the label far more often than ablating random units (§7), so the attributions are causally live even though the image's share of the decision is small.

What the image channel contains stays unresolved. Three probes tried to give the small image contribution a content and all three came back negative: the attribution lands slightly below an equal-area control inside printed-text boxes (§6), the concept probe fails its own validity checks (§6), and re-fitting the fusion head with modality dropout lowers the image's share rather than raising it (§8). Agreement with the human annotators moves in the pre-registered direction without reaching the threshold: ρ = -0.07 on the pre-registered hate-relevant subset and -0.05 once the target level is controlled, against a required -0.20 (§9). Two measurements were added late and change what the dataset looks like. A 200-image blind OCR gold set puts EasyOCR's detection rate at 70.9% with a 0% false-positive rate, so roughly half the corpus contains readable words rather than the third the raw coverage suggested (§1). The concept set turns out to have been built from detectors rather than a human pass; §6 measures it and §11 records the deviation. Negative results, including one silent bug that would have invalidated §3, are documented in §11.

2. Units, interventions, and what "faithful" is allowed to mean¶

The numbers in §3 depend on three definitions.

Units. Text explanations act on content BPE tokens: CLIP's tokenizer adds a start and an end sentinel, and those two are never units. Image explanations act on the 7×7 patch grid (32×32 pixels per cell), the resolution Grad-CAM actually has.

Deletion is an intervention on the mask, not on the ids. CLIP's text tower pools at the first end-of-sequence token found by argmax, so writing a pad id into a sentence silently moves the pooled position forward and truncates the tail, an intervention that changes two things at once. Deletion here sets attention_mask to 0 at the chosen positions and leaves input_ids untouched. On the image side, deletion writes the mean colour, which is exactly the "blank" counterfactual used in the coalition game, so §3 and §5 agree about what removing evidence means.

The missing-modality counterfactual is content-free but not zero. Text is replaced by the encoding of the empty string; the image is replaced by a mean-colour image. Both are real inputs the towers can process, which matters because a zero vector is off-manifold for CLIP's LayerNorm.

Faithfulness. A ranking is scored by AOPC: delete units in the order the method ranks them (highest first), measure the drop in the target at each of six fractions, and average. Insertion mirrors it, starting from the blank modality. The reported number is the mean of the deletion and insertion AOPC, so a method cannot win by ranking everything as "important". Every method is compared with a random ordering of the same units, averaged over repeats, by a paired test on the same samples.

The floors were fixed before the comparison ran: text AOPC > 0.01, image AOPC > 0.001. The ten-fold difference is deliberate: image attributions on CLIP-scale models are numerically much smaller, and a single floor would either pass noise on the image side or fail every method on the text side. The rule for naming a winner, also pre-registered: clear the floor and beat random; highest AOPC wins; a difference below 0.001 or a paired p above 0.05 is a tie, broken by comprehensiveness at 20%, then by seed/noise stability, then in favour of the deterministic method.

An explanation that also describes a randomly initialised network is describing the architecture, not the learned function. Each tower is randomised on its own: the last 0, 3, 6, 9 or all 12 encoder blocks re-initialised, the other tower and the fusion head untouched. 5. How much of the decision is the image? A two-player coalition game¶

Attribution methods disagree; the coalition game does not, because with two players the Shapley value has a closed form:

$$\phi_T = \tfrac12\big[v(T)-v(\varnothing)\big] + \tfrac12\big[v(T,I)-v(I)\big], \qquad \phi_I = \tfrac12\big[v(I)-v(\varnothing)\big] + \tfrac12\big[v(T,I)-v(T)\big]$$

where $v(\varnothing)$ is the blank-text/blank-image input, $v(T)$ is real text over a mean-colour image, $v(I)$ is empty text over the real image, and $v(T,I)$ is the actual input. The target is the four-head mean probability on 3,000 analysis samples. Completeness ($\phi_T + \phi_I = v(T,I) - v(\varnothing)$) holds to 6.9e-08, so the decomposition is exact rather than approximate.

| quantity | value | |---|---| | mean $\phi_T$ | +0.0467 | | median $\phi_T$ | +0.0152 | | share of samples with $\phi_T > 0$ | 54.6% | | mean $\phi_I$ | +0.0014 | | median $\phi_I$ | -0.0004 | | share of samples with $\phi_I < 0$ | 56.5% | | text-dominant samples ($|\phi_T| > |\phi_I|$) | 99.4% | | interaction $v(T,I)-v(T)-v(I)+v(\varnothing)$ | -0.0007 |

The text accounts for most of the decision. Mean $\phi_T$ is 34× the mean $\phi_I$. In a paired sense the image moves the target by about one percentage point, and that is the ceiling on any "the model uses the image" claim.

"Ignored" is the wrong word. $\phi_I$ is negative on 56.5% of samples: for a majority of inputs, replacing the picture with a blank one raises the hate probability. The image is not a neutral modality that the fusion gate happens to down-weight; it frequently pulls against the text's evidence. That asymmetry is invisible in accuracy numbers and invisible in gate values, and it is the strongest argument for doing the coalition game rather than reading the gate.

6. What the image channel contains¶

Two probes ask what the image contributes on the samples where it does contribute: a patch-level overlap test against printed text inside the picture, and a concept probe on the image tower. Both come back negative. The concept probe also fails its own label audit, which is a separate finding and is reported as such.

Patch attribution against printed text. Each 7×7 cell gets a text weight equal to the fraction of its area covered by an EasyOCR box, so a small box that only clips a cell still counts and no sample is dropped for having text too small to contain a cell centre. The control keeps the union area of the text boxes fixed and moves a square of that area to a random position, 100 placements, weighted the same way. The comparison is therefore "the same amount of image, placed on the text" against "placed elsewhere", not against zero.

quantity value
samples with at least one text box 1,113 of 3,000
mean area share of the boxes 8.0%

The excess is -0.5 pp against a pre-registered threshold of +15 pp, so the criterion is missed, and it is missed in the negative direction: attribution inside the text boxes is slightly below an equal-area control, and the paired test rejects equality that way (p = 2.1e-07). Only 7.3% of images put more attribution on the text than the control's 95th percentile. On this checkpoint the image tower is not re-reading the words inside the picture; if anything it looks away from them. A negative excess does not make text unimportant; it says that a text region draws no more attribution than any other patch of the same size. The control square matches the union of the text boxes; using the sum of the box areas instead, which double-counts overlapping boxes and makes the control larger, moves the excess to -1.1 pp, so the sign and the verdict do not depend on that choice.

Both halves of this design fail, independently.

The statistic is degenerate here. The per-sample gradient has a mean cosine of 0.830 with its own mean, so its direction barely varies across inputs. A sign test on a nearly fixed direction can only return 0 or 1, which is what the first two rows show, and the null betrays the same collapse: 20 random directions produce scores that jump between 0.0003 and 0.981 (sd 0.383). The near-constancy is mechanistic. With the four sigmoid heads saturated for most inputs, the derivative's direction comes from the fusion head's weights; the image input barely moves it.

The concept directions are weak. CAV accuracy is 0.62 for printed_text and face against balanced 400/400 sets, and 0.86 for flat_graphic. The printed_text case rules out bad annotation as the explanation: its labels are 0.93/0.94. The limit sits in the gradient.

The section's answer is therefore a double negative: no evidence that the image channel's contribution is text-in-image, and no concept-level claim that survives its own validity checks.

7. Failure modes: are the blamed units actually load-bearing?¶

An attribution can be faithful in a deletion curve and still be irrelevant to the decision. The sharper test: delete the top 10% of units the winner blames, recompute the decision set from the released thresholds, and ask whether the label changed. Text deletion flips the attention mask; image deletion writes the mean colour into whole cells. Each is compared with random deletion of the same number of units, three repeats, exact McNemar on the discordant pairs. n = 3000 analysis samples.

ablation flips random difference McNemar p
text 38.8% 11.1% +27.7 pp 5.3e-254
image 0.2% 0.1% +0.1 pp 6.9e-01
both 38.8% 11.1% +27.7 pp 5.8e-257

The threshold is a 5 pp difference with p < 0.05. The random arm is reduced to its majority vote across the three repeats before pairing, so each sample contributes one binary random outcome against the single top-k outcome. Note what the comparison is: top-10% text is about three tokens, top-10% image is about five cells, and on this model removing three well-chosen tokens is a much more violent intervention than removing five patch cells, which is the asymmetry §5 measured from the coalition side.

If the text ablation flips confident correct cases as easily as it flips false negatives, the explanation is describing the decision rule and not the mistake.

8. Forcing the model to use the image: a modality-level ROAR¶

Removing an important component should hurt. Applied at the modality level that logic becomes a statement about training rather than attribution: if text reliance is a training artefact, retraining the fusion head with modality dropout should raise the image's share without costing much accuracy. The encoders stay frozen and only the fusion block is touched, so the arms differ in one thing.

| arm | eval macro-F1 | train macro-F1 | image share of $|\phi|$ | image dominance | |---|---|---|---|---| | 1 released head | 0.576 | --- | 6.5% | 0.5% | | 2 re-initialised, refit on 2,000 | 0.461 ± 0.004 | 0.642 | 22.7% ± 9.6 | 15.0% | | 3 same, plus modality dropout p = 0.3 | 0.483 ± 0.002 | 0.558 | 11.3% ± 0.5 | 7.1% |

Three seeds per arm, 400 epochs, stratified 2,000/1,000 split inside the analysis set fixed across arms. Pre-registered: the image's share must rise while macro-F1 drops by at most 0.01. The dropout arm moves the share by -11.4 pp and macro-F1 by +0.0224, so the criterion fails, and it fails in the wrong direction. Modality dropout made the head rely on the image less, not more.

The mechanism is worth stating because it explains why the obvious fix does not work. Dropout replaces the modality feature with its blank value but leaves the presence flag at 1, so the head learns that this particular feature vector carries no information rather than that the image should be weighted up. At evaluation the real image still receives the same small weight. Buying image reliance needs the modality removed at the input and the label preserved, which is a different training objective.

What this comparison can and cannot support. The refit arms see 2,000 samples and reach an eval macro-F1 of 0.461 against 0.576 for the released head, so absolute numbers are not comparable and arm 1 is a scale reference only. Arm 2 against arm 3 shares data, capacity and optimiser, so that contrast is the interpretable one. The sign is stable across training budgets: at 150 / 400 / 800 epochs the image share moves 0.178→0.110, 0.227→0.113 and 0.206→0.120, and in every budget the dropout arm is the more accurate one (+2.6 / +2.2 / +2.1 pp macro-F1). Under a balanced objective the head does not merely fail to pick up the image; it does better by leaning on the text harder. A test at the original training scale is not available here, and §12 says so. One correction belongs here too: the first run of this experiment used an inverted class weight in the BCE (n_pos/n_neg instead of n_neg/n_pos), which down-weighted the rare heads and produced eval F1s near 0.38; the numbers above are from the corrected objective (§11).

9. Does the model read the same cues as people?¶

If the model leaned on visual context as much as humans do, samples where annotators agree should show a smaller text share than samples where they disagree: the model would be using the picture precisely where the words are ambiguous. The pre-registered primary analysis restricts to the hate-relevant subset, samples where at least one annotator gave a usable hate label (religion does not count as one): n = 2,202 of 3,000, with 798 all-none tweets excluded, because leaving them in turns the question into "normal tweets against hateful tweets". On that subset, agreement (1.0 / 0.67 / 0.33 from the raw annotations) against the text share by one-sided Spearman:

$$\rho = -0.071, \quad p_{\text{one-sided}} = 4.6e-04$$

The magnitude does not clear the -0.2 threshold by a wide margin, so the hypothesis is reported as a miss (§11). It also gets weaker under its own controls: partialling out the human usable-vote count and the $v(T,I)$ quartile leaves $\rho_{\text{partial}} = -0.045$ (p = 0.017). On the full 3,000 the raw correlation is -0.102, but under the same controls it is -0.008. Most of the uncontrolled association is agreement moving with the target level, not with the modality mix. The first version of this analysis ran the full-sample correlation without the controls and read the result as a directional near-miss; the restricted and controlled version is what is reported here, and it is weaker.

10. Three checks that could have broken the story¶

A3, preprocessing. The tweet text is fed raw: URLs, @mentions and RT markers included. The criterion was a shift below 10% with the dominance ordering unchanged: pass. Truncated URLs and @handles are not carrying the effect.

Target sensitivity, four heads against five. Dropping religion is a modelling choice, so the whole analysis is recomputed with all five heads averaged:

target mean $\phi_T$ mean $\phi_I$ text dominance
four usable heads +0.0467 +0.0014 99.4%
five heads +0.0267 +0.0015 96.4%

Dominance differs by 3.0 pp; the ordering is unchanged, which is why the four-head target is defensible.

A different checkpoint of the same idea (SigLIP2). The H1 replication swaps CLIP ViT-B/32 for google/siglip2-base-patch16-224 with the same gated-fusion head and five heads, on the same 3,000 analysis samples. Text is the larger modality on 94.8% of samples against 99.4% for the audited CLIP checkpoint, a gap of 4.6 pp against a pre-registered tolerance of 10 pp, so the pattern is not specific to one set of weights.

Two caveats decide how much that sentence is worth. The absolute values are not comparable: this fusion adds interaction terms, and both Shapley means come out negative ($\phi_T$ -0.0091, $\phi_I$ -0.0097), which means the blank baseline outscores the real input on average for this checkpoint. Only the share and the ordering are interpreted. And the released forward cannot run on transformers 5 as shipped: it passes a BaseModelOutputWithPooling straight into F.normalize. The fusion block is therefore reimplemented from the pooler tensors; a parity check against the checkpoint's own forward gives a maximum absolute difference of 0.0 on eight samples. The wording is "robust", not "reproduced": a different tokenizer, thresholds and feature width make this a check that the pattern generalises, not a replication of the same model.

11. What went wrong, including one bug that would have faked the result¶

Eleven negative or uncomfortable outcomes are reported here.

1. A silent bug made three text methods identical. The first P1.3 run produced LIME, KernelSHAP and the random ordering with the same AOPC to six decimals. The cause: content_positions expected a 1-D attention mask and returned an empty list for the (1, 77) tensor the tokenizer actually produces, so every text method fell back to in-order ranking and "did LIME beat random?" was measuring nothing. The tell was the exact equality across methods; the whole file was deleted and regenerated after the function was fixed to squeeze the batch dimension. Had it survived, §3 would have reported that no text method beats random on a model that is 99.4% text-driven.

2. The reference method is the worst text method. Occlusion ranks below random on text (+0.0013 versus +0.0179). Single-token deletions are strongly non-additive on this checkpoint, so a leave-one-out ranking disagrees with the model's own additive structure; LIME and KernelSHAP, which fit that structure, win. This is a caution against treating occlusion as a neutral ground truth.

3. The winner's metric is its own objective. AOPC rewards exactly what LIME optimises. The KernelSHAP margin of 0.0015 is small, and the honest statement is that both surrogate methods clear the floor, not that LIME is "the" faithful method.

4. A pre-registered hypothesis missed, and the first version of it was the wrong analysis. On the pre-registered hate-relevant subset the correlation is -0.071 against a required -0.20 (§9), and it shrinks to -0.045 once the usable-vote count and the target level are controlled. The number reported for two days was the full-sample correlation (-0.102), which under the same controls collapses to -0.008: most of it was agreement moving with the target level, not with the modality mix. The larger number is significant and correctly signed, which makes it attractive; the report keeps the restricted, controlled one and marks the hypothesis a miss.

5. The concept probe does not work, for a reason unrelated to labels. The per-sample gradients are 0.83-collinear, so the score saturates at 0 or 1 and the random-direction null does the same. The concept whose labels are demonstrably good (printed_text at 0.93/0.94) is also the one whose CAV only reaches 0.62 accuracy, which locates the failure in the gradient. This is reported as a methodological failure rather than as evidence about the model.

6. The intervention that should have worked did not. Modality dropout moved the image share by -11.4 pp against a pre-registered positive direction, while macro-F1 moved +0.022; the dropout head is simultaneously less image-reliant and more accurate. The refit is sample-limited (2,000 samples, §8), so the fair reading is that this cheap version of the fix fails, not that the fix is impossible.

7. The probe that should have supported the leakage story came back negative. The attribution inside printed-text boxes is -0.5 pp below an equal-area control (p = 2.1e-07), against a pre-registered +15 pp. The hypothesis that the image channel re-reads printed text is therefore not supported by the one measurement designed to find it.

8. The gold standard was missing for most of the project. EasyOCR coverage was reported for two days without any error estimate. A 200-image blind gold set fixed that late (§1): detection 70.9%, false-positive rate 0%, and an implied text-bearing share within 0.2 pp of the human rate. Any claim that X% of images contain Y needs its error rate measured when the claim is made, not after it has propagated into three other sections.

9. Detached runs died silently. Two long evaluations exited with no traceback, no error log and no Windows crash event, at roughly the same sample count. Re-running in the foreground with faulthandler completed both times. The pipeline therefore writes one JSON line per sample and resumes from the file, so a kill costs at most one sample; every long script here is restartable for that reason.

10. The first overlap control was geometrically larger than the thing it controlled for. The equal-area control sized its square from the sum of the OCR box areas while the observed statistic used the union of those boxes; where boxes overlap the sum exceeds the union (mean ratio 0.955, and below 0.98 for 46% of the 1,113 samples), so the control square was too big and the excess looked more negative than it is. Recomputing with a union-area control moves the excess from −1.16 pp to −0.48 pp (§6). The verdict is unchanged: it still misses +15 pp in the negative direction. The earlier number overstated the effect by a factor of 2.4, and both variants are now reported side by side.

11. The first ROAR objective inverted the class weights. pos_weight in the BCE was set to n_pos/n_neg, which down-weights the rare heads (religion at 2.7% prevalence got a weight near 0.1) and pushed both refit arms to an eval macro-F1 near 0.38. Corrected to n_neg/n_pos the arms reach 0.461 and 0.483, and the comparison becomes sharper rather than softer: dropout now costs nothing in accuracy while still cutting the image share (§8). A wrong objective that makes both arms look bad is worse than a wrong objective that makes one arm look bad, because it invites the reader to blame the sample size.

12. Conclusions¶

The answer to the research question. On clip-vit-base-mmhs150k-mtl, the text modality accounts for 99.4% of the decision in a two-player Shapley decomposition, with mean $\phi_T$ = +0.0467 against $\phi_I$ = +0.0014. The image is used, but weakly and often adversely: $\phi_I < 0$ on 56.5% of samples. Ablating the top 10% of blamed text units flips 38.8% of decisions against 11.1% for random units, which is what makes the attributions more than a description of the input.

Which explainers can be trusted. Only methods that were checked against deletion and against a randomised model are used for the interpretation: LIME and KernelSHAP on text, occlusion on image. Grad-CAM, integrated gradients and Score-CAM fail the image floor on this checkpoint, and occlusion fails the text floor. Both facts are results about the model-plus-method pair, not about the methods in general.

What the image channel is not. Three probes tried to give the small image contribution a content, and all three came back negative. The image attribution inside printed-text boxes is -0.5 pp below an equal-area control, so the leakage hypothesis is not supported. The concept probe cannot adjudicate: its gradients are 0.83-collinear so the score saturates, two of its three concept sets are contaminated on the negative side, and the one clean concept still yields a 0.62-accuracy CAV (§6). Re-fitting the fusion head with modality dropout moved the image's share by -11.4 pp while macro-F1 moved +0.0224: the head became less image-reliant and more accurate at once, so the cheap version of the fix is worse than useless.

Why the gate cannot be the explanation. The fused gate correlates with the Shapley split at ρ = +0.45 (p = 5.1e-149): statistically real, practically useless for a per-sample claim, and sign agreement with $\phi_T$ is only 86.2%. A model card that reports gate averages as evidence of multimodal use would be overclaiming.

The measurement the dataset does not ship. MMHS150K provides no OCR for this split, and EasyOCR's raw coverage (36.6%) is a lower bound: a 200-image blind gold set puts the detection rate at 70.9% with a 0% false-positive rate, and the implied true text-bearing share (51.6%) matches the human rate (51.8%) to 0.2 pp. That number matters for anyone who plans to study "text in images" here: roughly half the corpus contains readable words, and the standard detector finds seven in ten of them.

What would change the verdict. A checkpoint trained with modality dropout on the full training set, which this project could not run at scale (§8); and a dataset whose images carry labels the text does not. Until then the defensible engineering recommendation is to report the per-modality ablation next to the accuracy numbers, because a 0.99 text share is invisible in a macro-F1 of 0.6536.
